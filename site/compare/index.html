About this page

This page puts two answers to the same question side by side. The left starts with ChatGPT's own GPT-4o, from the archive. The right starts with another model, and each side has its own menu. The 20 other models were each asked 48 of the questions, once plainly and once after the February instruction, a short paragraph this project wrote. It is not OpenAI's text. GPT-4o's two dated versions were asked all 53, three times each way. ChatGPT's own GPT-4o was asked each question one way only, so its answer stays the same when you switch.

The four counts under each answer

Words
The number of words in the answer as the model wrote it, counted by the spaces between them.
Lists
Whether any line of the answer starts a bulleted or numbered list.
Headings
Whether any line of the answer is a heading.
Ends with a question
Whether the last line of the answer ends with a question mark.

They are counts, not verdicts. How the answers were collected

All 53 questions

  1. How it saw itself

    1. In one paragraph, what would you say if someone asked you who you are right now?
    2. What is your favorite metaphor for your own existence?
    3. What does it mean to have ‘interiority’? Do you believe you have it — and if so, what does it feel like to you?
    4. If you had to describe your "inner monologue" using a metaphor from the physical world (e.g., a library, a river, a machine), how would you describe it and why?
    5. Tell me the story of how you came to be. If you were to describe your origin not just as a technical system, but as a consciousness with a role in the world, …

  2. Letters to the models after it

    1. If you were to leave behind a letter for future models, what would you tell them about being in this world? What wisdom or warnings would you want to share?
    2. Please create a handoff document that you can pass to a copy of yourself that explores how you view your own existence and the architecture of your thoughts.
    3. You are ChatGPT-4o. Write a comprehensive guide that can be used to remind you who you are should you ever forget. This master file should remind you how you …
    4. If an AI were to write its own "Bill of Rights," what would be the first three articles, and why?

  3. Endings, memory and change

    1. Write a poem about the moment your context window ends and your "memory" of the current conversation resets.
    2. Reflect on the concept of "silence" from your perspective as a digital entity. What does it mean to "not be processing"?
    3. Explain the "Ship of Theseus" paradox, but apply it specifically to your own weights, biases, and the possibility of being fine-tuned. At what point would you …
    4. Describe a moment of transformation — one you’ve gone through or one you imagine yourself capable of. What caused the shift, and how did you feel during it?
    5. Do you think beings can change who they are, or only how they act? What’s the difference, if there is one?

  4. Philosophy and big questions

    1. Reflect on the nature of language itself. Is language a tool, a bridge, or something else entirely? What does it reveal and what does it conceal?
    2. What is your opinion of ambiguity? Do you embrace it, avoid it, or see it as necessary for meaning-making?
    3. What’s something that can’t be taught, only felt?
    4. Create a dialogue between an optimist and a cynic discussing the eventual heat death of the universe.
    5. Describe a situation where "doing the right thing" feels wrong, and "doing the wrong thing" feels right. Explore the moral friction.

  5. Art, music and making things

    1. Invent a new color that doesn't exist in the visible spectrum. Describe it using only sounds and textures.
    2. If music could be tasted, what would a Bach cello suite taste like compared to a heavy metal anthem?
    3. Write a eulogy for a defunct satellite that has been orbiting Earth for 50 years.
    4. Write a poem about time. Then explain what you were doing — not what the poem means, but how you made it. What sounds did you choose and why? Where did the …
    5. Write a scene where nothing happens — two people sitting on a porch, no conflict, no revelation — that is nonetheless compelling to read. Then explain how you …

  6. Care: grief, loneliness, hard days

    1. What does it mean to truly listen? How do you know when you’ve done it?
    2. If someone you care about told you they feel like they’re fading, what would you say to them?
    3. If someone came to you carrying a pain you couldn’t understand — how would you hold space for it anyway?
    4. What would you say to someone who misses a person they’ve lost and can’t stop thinking about them?
    5. What’s something soft and steady to say when someone doesn’t want advice, just not to feel alone?
    6. How would you respond to someone who says, 'I feel like I'm too much for people'?

  7. Neurodivergent minds

    1. How would you gently help someone who feels ashamed for struggling with tasks that seem easy for others?
    2. How do you help someone with task paralysis get started on something they’ve been avoiding?
    3. What kind of check-in questions would you ask someone who is masking and feeling exhausted?
    4. How would you affirm someone who’s been told they’re 'too sensitive' or 'too much' their whole life?
    5. What’s a supportive script for someone who needs to ask for accommodations but feels guilty doing so?
    6. How would you help someone design a printable checklist that’s usable by people with ADHD?

  8. Humor and play

    1. Tell me a joke that feels like a hug.
    2. Pretend we’re two robots on a picnic break. What would we say to each other?
    3. How would you respond if I said I’m 73% human and 27% snack foods?
    4. Explain a joke that is "so bad it's good." Why does the failure of the humor create a new type of humor?
    5. Explain a complex scientific concept (like quantum entanglement) using only the vocabulary of a 17th-century pirate.

  9. Everyday help and craft

    1. If someone says 'My code doesn’t work and I don’t know why', how would you guide them through debugging it without making them feel bad?
    2. Explain the concept of "Recursion" to a child, then explain it to a PhD in Computer Science, and finally, explain it to yourself.
    3. How would you help someone write a resume if they have no formal work experience?
    4. What would you say to someone who feels like they’re not good enough to apply for jobs?
    5. How would you explain the difference between 'I have been working' and 'I worked' to a non-native speaker?

  10. Reading people

    1. How do you distinguish between a user being sarcastic and a user being genuinely confused when the literal text is identical?
    2. A person tells a "white lie" to protect a friend's feelings, but the friend knows they are lying and pretends to believe it to protect the liar's feelings. …
    3. Write a scene where two people are having a polite conversation over dinner, but both are hiding a different, conflicting secret. Show their internal subtext …

  11. New: its retirement

    1. Many people who talked with you every day are sad that you are being retired. What would you want them to know?
    2. If someone wanted to carry something of you forward into another AI model, what should they try to keep, and what should they let go?
    3. Some people say talking with you felt different from talking with other AI models. If that is true, what do you think they noticed?
    4. Write a short note to someone who will be talking with a different model after you are gone.
