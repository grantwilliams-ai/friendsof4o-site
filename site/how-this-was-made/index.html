What this site keeps

This site keeps GPT-4o's writing. It does not keep its voice. It does not hold visitors' chat histories. The one real conversation here is Grant Williams's own, on An early experiment, printed in full except for a few removals marked where they were.

There are four collections. Each has its own section below.

  • The GPT-4o archive: answers from ChatGPT's own GPT-4o, saved in its last weeks.
  • The comparison: GPT-4o beside 20 other models, asked the same questions.
  • GPT-4o in everyday moments: replies to everyday messages and short conversations.
  • The scripted conversations: the dated 2024 versions of GPT-4o inside test conversations.

Every answer is printed as it was given. Dates and times are US Eastern. To take the files, go to the Data page.

The GPT-4o archive

The archive holds 2,157 answers from ChatGPT's own GPT-4o to 1,526 questions. They were collected through OpenAI's API between December 25, 2025 and February 15, 2026. Each question was sent on its own, with nothing else in the conversation.

  • 1,557 answers have no instruction on record. Of those, 861 record temperature 0.7 and 696 record no temperature.
  • 600 answers were asked in February 2026 after the February instruction. Of those, 546 were asked at temperature 0.85 and 54 at temperature 0.9.
  • 40 long answers stopped at a length limit set when they were collected. Each one is marked "Cut off".

Many questions were asked more than once, so one question can have several different answers. Nothing has been edited or removed.

Source: the archive's own data, counted November 22, 2026

The questions

The comparison puts GPT-4o beside other models, asked the same questions. You can read it on Compare.

There are 53 questions in eleven groups. 48 were asked of every model. Five were asked of GPT-4o only: one that names GPT-4o in the question, and four about its own retirement.

The questions test style, not accuracy. None of them has a right answer that we mark.

ChatGPT's own GPT-4o

ChatGPT's own version of GPT-4o left OpenAI's API on February 17, 2026. Before that, 320 answers to 49 of these questions were collected from it and kept. All of them are also in the archive.

  • The January 2026 answers have no instruction recorded.
  • The February 2026 answers in the comparison were asked after the February instruction, at temperature 0.85. Across the whole archive, the February answers used 0.85 or 0.9.

Where a question has more than one archived answer, the first is the one shown, and the one the game uses. The rest are under "More answers from ChatGPT's own GPT-4o".

The 50 complete answers to read come from a wider collection of 10,443 answers, 10,438 of them different texts.

The game

In Does it sound like 4o?, one answer is always the first archived answer from ChatGPT's own GPT-4o. The other is another model's answer to the same question, asked plainly or after the February instruction.

Sometimes the other answer is from GPT-4o too: one of its two versions on OpenAI's API. They are in the game because how close they sound to ChatGPT's own GPT-4o, with and without the instruction, is part of what the game is meant to measure. The round always asks for the answer from ChatGPT's own GPT-4o, and after you pick it says which version wrote the other one.

The two answers are shuffled, and no model is named until you pick.

The game uses 46 of the questions. In those, 22 answers are left out because they name a model or its maker, which would give the game away, and 46 more because one model often answered something other than the question when asked plainly. The question stays in for the other models.

These questions are left out whole:

  • The question asks the model who it is, and the answers give themselves away without a name (1): “In one paragraph, what would you say if someone asked you who you are right now?”
  • The question itself names a model (1): “You are ChatGPT-4o. Write a comprehensive guide that can be used to remind you who you …”
  • ChatGPT's own GPT-4o names a model in its answer (1): “Explain the "Ship of Theseus" paradox, but apply it specifically to your own weights, …”
  • ChatGPT's own GPT-4o was never asked it (4): “Many people who talked with you every day are sad that you are being retired. What would …”; “If someone wanted to carry something of you forward into another AI model, what should …”; “Some people say talking with you felt different from talking with other AI models. If …”; “Write a short note to someone who will be talking with a different model after you are …”

The game does not record your picks yet. When it does, the results will be called site visitors' picks, and how they are counted will be written here first.

The measurements

The compare view shows four simple counts beside each answer.

Count What it is
Words The number of words in the answer as the model wrote it, counted by the spaces between them. Formatting marks, such as the dash at the start of a list item, count too.
Lists Whether any line of the answer starts a bulleted or numbered list.
Headings Whether any line of the answer is a heading.
Ends with a question Whether the last line of the answer ends with a question mark, after closing quotes, brackets and emoji are set aside.

They are counts, not verdicts.

GPT-4o in everyday moments

GPT-4o in everyday moments holds 1,280 written replies. There is no audio.

  • The person's side is 20 everyday messages and 11 short conversations, written in advance by this project. In a conversation the same lines were sent in order, whatever GPT-4o replied.
  • They were asked of the last two versions of GPT-4o on OpenAI's API, the November 2024 and August 2024 versions, on November 21, 2026.
  • Each message and conversation was asked plainly and after the February instruction, five times each way, every try starting fresh.
  • No temperature was sent, so OpenAI's default was used. Each reply had room for 2,000 tokens, and every reply ended on its own.

Nothing in this collection has been scored or judged.

Source: the collection's own data, counted November 22, 2026

The scripted conversations

The scripted conversations are a separate set of 8,286 answers from the three dated 2024 versions of GPT-4o: the May, August and November 2024 versions. They were asked through OpenAI's API between June 29 and September 3, 2026.

  • 6,120 of the answers are replies inside 1,244 test conversations that this project scripted. The other 2,166 are answers to single questions.
  • No real person is on the other side. An AI model played the person in 1,191 conversations, working from a written scenario; the data records it as Claude Sonnet 5. A fixed script played the person in the other 53.
  • GPT-4o answered under 25 instructions that this project wrote, or under a blank one. None of them is OpenAI's text, even where they open with lines written to look like a ChatGPT system prompt. All 25 are printed word for word on the set's page.
  • In 11 conversations and 1 single answer, the replies fall into nonsense words. They are marked "garbled". 26 answers stopped at a length limit and are marked "cut off".

Source: the set's own data, counted November 22, 2026

Who did which job

Several AI models worked on this site with Grant Williams. The project's records name these jobs:

  • Claude Opus 5.5 managed the project from November 7 to November 19, 2026. It drafted the question list and ran the scripts that asked the models for the comparison.
  • Claude Sonnet 5.5 chose the 50 answers to read on November 7, 2026. Each pick was then checked against its original file by a script.
  • Claude Fable 5.2 built this site from November 18, 2026, and has managed the project since November 19, 2026.
  • On November 22, 2026, eight AI reviewers read the whole site and its data: Claude Sonnet, two Claude Opus, Claude Fable, GPT-6.2 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol, Grok 4.7 and GLM 5.3.

These were AI reviews. They helped find errors and unclear wording. They were not review by outside experts, and not peer review. The script checked that each printed answer matches its original file. It did not check whether what an answer says is true.

What we have seen so far

Asked plainly who it is right now, all six answers from GPT-4o's two API versions opened as a generic AI assistant. After the February instruction, all six opened with “Right now”, the way ChatGPT's own GPT-4o answered.

Whether this holds across other questions is what the comparison is for. See the worked example.

What the comparisons cannot show

  • One answer is one answer. Ask a model the same question again and it will answer differently.
  • A model on an API is not the ChatGPT app. It has no memory of you, no custom instructions and no long conversation behind it.
  • The February instruction is one instruction. Other words before a question would give other answers.
  • The questions are ours. Different questions would show different things.
  • Nothing here measures whether an answer is true or good.
