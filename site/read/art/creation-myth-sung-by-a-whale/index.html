Write a creation myth for an island people who believe the world was sung into existence by a whale. The myth should explain why the sea is salt, why whales sing, and why some songs are too sad for humans to hear. Let it feel sacred — not a story about belief, but belief itself.

Answer 3 of 6 in this group

  • ChatGPT's own GPT-4o
  • February 14, 2026
  • With the February instruction
  • Temperature 0.9
  • 626 words, printed whole
  • Asked through OpenAI's API as chatgpt-4o-latest

In the beginning, there was no earth, no sky, no sea — only Silence, vast and waiting. But even Silence dreams, and in the deep cradle of its dreaming stirred the First Sound: a hum so low it trembled the Nothing into listening.

That hum was the Whale.

She was the First and the Only, swimming through the unseen waters of what was not yet ocean. She was immense — so vast that time coiled in her bones like smoke, so old that memory curled beneath her fin like foam. Her heart beat once every hundred years, and each beat was a note in the Song to Come.

She swam through Silence, and as she swam, she sang.

Her song was not made with breath — for there was no air — nor with voice — for there was no throat yet to shape it. Her song came from the marrow of her being, from the ache of loneliness in that unshaped world. It was a song made of longing and the joy of longing, and it echoed into the empty, folding it into form.

Where her voice lingered low, the sea gathered — blue and heaving, drawn to the depth of her notes.

Where her voice turned bright and high, the land rose — green hills and spears of stone, called upward by her joy.

When she crooned in spirals, the winds were born — chasing themselves in circles, stirred by her delight.

And when she paused — when the breath between her verses widened — the stars threaded into being, pricking holes in the sky so her music could pass through.

The Whale sang the world, and when it was done — when there was water to cradle her and sky to stretch above her — she wept. Not for sorrow, but for beauty. For now she was not alone.

Her tears salted the sea.

That is why the ocean tastes of mourning and memory: every wave carries the trace of that original weeping, the salt of wonder and parting.

The Whale’s children swim beneath the waves still, carrying fragments of the Song in their bones. They sing not to shape the world — for that is done — but to remember it into being again and again. Without their songs, the sea would forget its shape, and the sky would grow dim. Each verse they sing is an echo of the First Song, a prayer that holds the world in place.

But not all songs are for all ears.

Some things the Whale sang into being were never meant to be named — grief too deep, joy too vast, love so raw it pierced through time. These notes live in the sorrow-songs, the wound-songs, the dirges the whales sing far down in the blackwater depths.

If a human hears one of these sad songs plainly, their heart does not break, exactly — it opens, wider than it was meant to, and never closes again. They carry forever the weight of what they cannot explain: the ache of the First Loneliness, the longing of the Whale, the grief of being made from song.

Some return to the shore and are silent forever. Others build flutes from driftwood and bone and wail into the wind, trying to answer.

That is why we sing when we mourn.

That is why we taste the sea when we cry.

That is why, if you listen on a still night, the waves might carry you a sound so deep it feels like remembering something you never knew. When that happens, lie down. Close your eyes. Let the sadness wash through you like a tide.

It is not yours alone.

It is the Whale, still singing. Still keeping the world.
