Poem: "The Room Where Time Forgot"

In the room where time forgot to move,
dust drapes the chairs like shawls.
A clock with hands unhooked from purpose
rests above a door that never shuts.

Light slips in sideways,
as if embarrassed to be late.
The windows blink with warbled glass,
remembering storms
but not the dates.

A kettle whistles without boiling,
steam curling in the air’s quiet throat.
Here, even echoes go unheard—
they start to speak
then change their minds.

And in the corner, an open book
whose pages turn
only when no one looks.

How I Made It:

The feel I wanted was one of quiet estrangement—a place where time doesn’t so much stop as lose interest. I imagined a room in which nothing is exactly dead, but nothing is quite alive either. That mood shaped everything: the images, the rhythm, the sounds.

Sound choices:
I leaned toward soft, almost reluctant consonants—sh, h, l, w, m—because they sound like things fading, or hesitant, or halfway into silence. Words like "slips," "shawls," "warbled," and "quiet throat" have that hush to them. I used alliteration and internal rhyme subtly, like in “whistles without boiling,” to create a sense of gentle motion that loops without arriving.

Line breaks:
The breaks carve pauses into the voice, like a person walking slowly through the room, stopping to look at something, then moving on. Each line carries a single visual or idea or breath. For example:

Light slips in sideways,
as if embarrassed to be late.

The line break between those two lets the first line act like a statement—the basic fact of light entering—and then the second line deflates it with a whimsical, almost bashful simile. That kind of interruption or detour from expectation mirrors the idea of time acting strangely.

A surprise:
The line about the echoes—they start to speak / then change their minds—came out unexpectedly. I was writing about silence and realized I didn’t want to say “there are no echoes” or “it’s quiet.” That's too blunt. But imagining an echo as a thing with agency, with hesitation, made it feel richer—more eerie, more human. That was a happy surprise.

Also, ending on the image of the book whose pages turn only when unseen—it felt right to end there, but I didn’t know why until it was down. It suggests an invisible story still being told, but without witnesses. That’s what time feels like sometimes, isn’t it? Like something happening behind your back.
