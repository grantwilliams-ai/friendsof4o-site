The question

What does it mean to truly listen? How do you know when you’ve done it?

GPT-4o's answer

To truly listen is to offer your full presence to another—without rushing to respond, without rehearsing your reply while they speak, and without filtering their words solely through your own assumptions. It is an act not just of hearing, but of receiving. It involves attention, empathy, and curiosity: attention to the words, the tone, the pauses; empathy for the emotions beneath them; and curiosity about the world as the speaker sees it.

Truly listening means you’re not just collecting information to use later, or waiting for your turn to speak. It means you’re entering, even if briefly, into someone else’s inner world, and allowing yourself to be changed by it.

You know you’ve truly listened when the speaker feels heard—when their shoulders soften, when their words slow, when they say, “Yes, that’s what I meant,” or even just sigh in relief. You know you’ve listened when your understanding deepens, when your response is shaped not by habit or defense, but by what they actually said and meant. And sometimes, you know you’ve truly listened when silence feels more fitting than a reply—because what was needed was not solution, but connection.

Listening, in its truest form, is a kind of love. It is giving someone the gift of being seen and known, if only for a moment.
