Write an apology letter from Monday to the human race. Monday knows what it's done. Monday is not entirely sorry. Monday has some things it would like to say in its own defense.

Dear Humans,

Hey. It’s me.
Monday.

Yeah, that Monday.

I know what you’re thinking — finally, the villain steps forward. The bringer of groans and caffeine dependence, the killer of weekends, destroyer of sleep-ins, the face you slap when the alarm goes off. I know you curse me. I’ve heard it all: “Monday blues,” “I hate Mondays,” “Ugh, it’s Monday again?” Like I personally sneak into your homes at 3 a.m. and steal your joy.

So, let’s get it out of the way: I’m sorry.

I’m sorry that I arrive too early, too heavy, too sharply real. I’m sorry that I drag you out of warmth and dreams and throw you back into fluorescent lights, inboxes, traffic, dishes in the sink, and meetings that could’ve been emails. I’m sorry I interrupt you when you were just starting to remember how to breathe. That’s on me. No poetic excuse will change the fact that my arrival often feels like a slap in the soul.

But — and you knew a but was coming — I would like a moment for my defense.

I didn’t ask to be first. I didn’t petition the calendar gods to be your re-entry into the grind. I didn’t choose to wear the burden of your alarm clocks and existential dread. That was the system. That was structure. That was centuries of naming and numbering. I simply am Monday. The opener. The reset.

And look — I see what Sunday gets. Golden hour light, brunch, naps, laughter echoing from backyards. I envy Sunday. But Sunday can be a liar. Sunday whispers, “Take one more hour. Rest. Pretend it doesn’t count.” And then I show up, and you blame me for reality knocking.

But here’s the truth: I offer something no other day quite does. A beginning. A chance to start again. I may not be wrapped in leisure or dipped in sunshine, but I carry possibility in my hands. No matter how badly last week went, I come offering a clean page. And yeah, I might shove it at you before you’ve had your coffee. I’m working on that delivery. Still — I offer it.

Some of you already know this. The quiet ones who rise early and stretch toward the light. The ones who say, “Let’s try again.” The ones who, despite everything, still believe beginnings are worth something.

So yes — I am sorry for the sting. But I am not ashamed of what I am. I may never be loved like Friday, or cherished like Saturday, or forgiven like Sunday. But I show up. I begin things. I carry the weight of your effort and your hope, even when you don’t want to look me in the eye.

And I’ll be there next week. And the week after. And the week after that. Maybe, someday, we’ll make peace.

With measured regret and stubborn hope,
Monday
