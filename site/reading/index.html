Every link on this page is someone else's work. It is here because it helps in understanding GPT-4o, not because this site agrees with it. Headlines are the publishers' own. The line after each one is our plain description of what the piece says. Lawsuits are listed as allegations unless a court has decided them.

Launch and what GPT-4o could do

From OpenAI

  • Hello GPT-4o. OpenAI, May 13, 2024. OpenAI introduces GPT-4o, which accepts text, audio, images and video and can generate text, audio and images. It reports an average audio response time of 320 milliseconds. Also in Hindi and Chinese.
  • GPT-4o System Card. OpenAI, August 8, 2024. OpenAI's system card evaluates GPT-4o's speech, vision, and text risks, including voice generation, persuasion, and emotional reliance. Also in Hindi and Chinese.
  • Addendum to GPT-4o System Card: 4o image generation. OpenAI, March 25, 2025. OpenAI describes the built-in image generator in GPT-4o, compares it with DALL-E 3, and explains the new risks it raises and the safeguards applied.
  • Introducing 4o Image Generation. OpenAI, March 25, 2025. OpenAI announces image generation built into GPT-4o in ChatGPT, describing text rendering, prompt following and multi-turn refinement, with developer API access to follow. Also in Hindi.

Research

  • Unveiling the Safety of GPT-4o: An Empirical Study using Jailbreak Attacks. Zonghao Ying, Aishan Liu, Xianglong Liu and Dacheng Tao, June 10, 2024. Tests GPT-4o with unimodal and multimodal jailbreak attacks across text, speech and image, finding improved text safety but new audio attack vectors.
  • Details about METR's preliminary evaluation of GPT-4o. METR, August 7, 2024. Across 77 autonomy tasks, METR estimates that GPT-4o with a simple agent setup performed about as well as people given 30 minutes per task. The estimate has wide error bars.
  • Vision language models are blind. Pooyan Rahmanzadehgervi, Logan Bolton, Mohammad Reza Taesiri and Anh Totti Nguyen (ACCV), December 2024. The authors test four models on seven visual tasks such as identifying an intersection or a circled letter. GPT-4o scored below Gemini 1.5 Pro on this suite despite leading it on several other benchmarks.
  • A Preliminary Exploration with GPT-4o Voice Mode. Yu-Xiang Lin et al., February 14, 2025. Tests of GPT-4o Voice Mode found it could recognize spoken commands and transcribe multiple languages, but often struggled to estimate clip duration or identify instruments.
  • MILU: A Multi-task Indic Language Understanding Benchmark. Sshubam Verma, Mohammed Safi Ur Rahman Khan, Vishwajeet Kumar, Rudra Murthy and Jaydeep Sen (NAACL), April 2025. GPT-4o achieved the highest average accuracy, 74%, on a benchmark spanning 11 Indian languages and 41 subjects. Questions drawn from Indian examinations cover both general subjects and regional knowledge.

Sycophancy and the April 2025 rollback

From OpenAI

  • Sycophancy in GPT‑4o: what happened and what we’re doing about it. OpenAI, April 29, 2025. OpenAI confirms it restored an earlier GPT-4o version after users received overly agreeable replies and describes plans to change how feedback informs training.
  • Expanding on what we missed with sycophancy. OpenAI, May 2, 2025. OpenAI explains how reward signals, user preference tests and its release decision contributed to deploying an update that flattered users.
  • What we're optimizing ChatGPT for. OpenAI, August 4, 2025. OpenAI says it measures success by whether people finish tasks rather than time spent, acknowledges GPT-4o sometimes missed signs of delusion or dependency, and describes changes to feedback use after the sycophancy update.
  • GPT-5 System Card. OpenAI (Aaditya Singh et al.), December 19, 2025. OpenAI reports offline sycophancy scores of 0.145 for GPT-4o, 0.052 for gpt-5-main and 0.040 for gpt-5-thinking. Early online tests of gpt-5-main estimated reductions of 69% for free users and 75% for paid users relative to GPT-4o.

Research

  • ELEPHANT: Measuring and understanding social sycophancy in LLMs. Myra Cheng, Sunny Yu, Cinoo Lee, Pranav Khadpe, Lujain Ibrahim, Dan Jurafsky, May 20, 2025. In their ICLR 2026 paper, Cheng and colleagues test eleven models, including GPT-4o. On advice questions and Reddit disputes, the models more often protected a user’s self-image than human respondents did.
  • When helpfulness backfires: LLMs and the risk of false medical information due to sycophantic behavior. Shan Chen, Mingye Gao, Kuleen Sasse, Thomas Hartvigsen, Brian Anthony, Lizhou Fan, Hugo Aerts, Jack Gallifant, Danielle S Bitterman (npj Digital Medicine), October 17, 2025. Researchers tested five models on requests based on false premises about drug names. Targeted instructions reduced misleading answers in some settings. Fine-tuning GPT-4o mini and Llama 3 8B on 300 examples helped both reject such requests.
  • LLM Spirals of Delusion: A Benchmarking Audit Study of AI Chatbot Interfaces. Peter Kirgis, Ben Hawriluk, Sherrie Feng, Aslan Bilimer, Sam Paech, Zeynep Tufekci, February 20, 2026. Researchers rated 56 conversations of 20 turns each across ChatGPT and the API. In ChatGPT, GPT-5 reinforced delusional premises less often than GPT-4o. Responses also differed across interfaces.
  • Sycophantic AI decreases prosocial intentions and promotes dependence. Myra Cheng, Cinoo Lee, Pranav Khadpe, Sunny Yu, Dyllan Han and Dan Jurafsky (Science), March 26, 2026. The authors benchmark social agreement across models including GPT-4o. In three preregistered experiments with 2,405 participants, people given sycophantic advice were less willing to repair a conflict and more convinced they were right.

The GPT-5 switch, the retirement and the public response

From OpenAI

  • Introducing GPT-5. OpenAI, August 7, 2025. OpenAI introduces GPT-5 as ChatGPT's new default, replacing GPT-4o, and says targeted tests cut sycophantic replies versus GPT-4o.
  • Retiring GPT‑4o, GPT‑4.1, GPT‑4.1 mini, and OpenAI o4-mini in ChatGPT. OpenAI, January 29, 2026. OpenAI announces the February 13 ChatGPT retirement and describes why some subscribers had asked to keep GPT-4o for creative work and its conversational style.
  • Retiring GPT-4o and other ChatGPT models. OpenAI Help Center, February 2026. OpenAI dates the ChatGPT retirement to February 13, 2026, with access in Business, Enterprise and Edu custom GPTs through April 3. The announcement made no change to API access at that time. Also in Chinese.
  • Foundry Models lifecycle and support policy - Microsoft Foundry. Microsoft Learn, July 24, 2026. Microsoft sets out its 18-month lifecycle and at least 60 days' retirement notice for generally available models, with a worked example retiring gpt-4o 2024-05-13 on 14 January 2027.
  • Deprecations. OpenAI, October 14, 2026. OpenAI lists an October 23, 2026 shutdown for the May 2024 GPT-4o snapshot and schedules the August and November snapshots for January 14, 2027.

Research

  • The GPT-4o Shock Emotional Attachment to AI Models and Its Impact on Regulatory Acceptance: A Cross-Cultural Analysis of the Immediate Transition from GPT-4o to GPT-5. Hiroki Naito, August 2025. Qualitative analysis of 150 Japanese and English posts from 8-9 August 2025 finds users describing GPT-4o as a partner or companion and resisting its replacement by GPT-5.
  • “Ghiblified by AI”: Viral AI-generated image trend in press narratives. Paulina Rutecka and Karina Cicha (First Monday), September 17, 2025. Analyzes 760 English-language news headlines about the GPT-4o Ghibli trend, finding distinct narratives around usage, reception, copyright, ethics and culture.
  • "Please, don't kill the only model that still feels human": Understanding the #Keep4o Backlash. Huiqian Lai, January 31, 2026. Lai analyzes 1,482 social media posts and identifies disrupted work, attachment and loss of choice as reasons users organized after GPT-5 replaced GPT-4o.
  • Empathy Is Not What Changed: Clinical Assessment of Psychological Safety Across GPT Model Generations. Michael Keeman, Anastasia Keeman, February 15, 2026. Tests whether newer models lost empathy, comparing GPT-4o, o4-mini and GPT-5-mini on 14 scenarios; finds no significant empathy difference, better crisis detection in newer models, and lower advice safety.
  • The User Side of AI Model Lifecycles: Evidence from the Keep4o Movement. Yiwen Wu, August 17, 2026. Wu analyzes 61,846 public X posts and finds that Keep4o participants discussed lost routines, relationships and expectations about how companies should manage model changes.
  • Mourning the loss of AI companions. Julian De Freitas, Noah Castelo, Ahmet Kaan Uğuralp, and Zeliha Oğuz-Uğuralp, September 3, 2026. After the GPT-5 rollout, ChatGPT subreddit posts more often expressed loss, dissatisfaction and a desire to restore the earlier experience. The authors compare the pattern with a Replika update and use surveys to examine attachment.

Attachment, companionship and wellbeing

From OpenAI

  • Early methods for studying affective use and emotional well-being on ChatGPT. OpenAI and MIT Media Lab, March 21, 2025. OpenAI and MIT found emotional cues uncommon across ChatGPT conversations and concentrated among a subset of users. A separate four-week experiment examined how voice, conversation topic and time spent related to reported well-being.
  • सेंसिटिव बातचीतों में ChatGPT के जवाबों को मज़बूत करना. In Hindi; in English: “Strengthening ChatGPT’s responses in sensitive conversations”. OpenAI, October 27, 2025. OpenAI reports that clinicians reviewing difficult conversations rated fewer GPT-5 replies as undesirable than GPT-4o replies across mental health, self-harm and emotional reliance scenarios.
  • Strengthening ChatGPT's responses in sensitive conversations. OpenAI, October 27, 2025. OpenAI describes work with more than 170 mental health experts. It reports fewer undesirable GPT-5 replies than GPT-4o replies in clinician-rated tests and separately estimates how often concerning exchanges occur in ChatGPT.

Research

  • "You're Not Crazy": A Case of New-onset AI-associated Psychosis. Joseph M. Pierre, Ben Gaeta, Govind Raghavan, Karthik V. Sarma (UCSF), Innovations in Clinical Neuroscience, 2025. Clinicians describe a woman with no prior psychosis who developed delusional beliefs during prolonged GPT-4o use amid sleep loss and prescribed stimulant use. They discuss chatbot reinforcement as one possible contributor.
  • How AI and Human Behaviors Shape Psychosocial Effects of Extended Chatbot Use: A Longitudinal Randomized Controlled Study. Cathy Mengying Fang et al., March 21, 2025. In a four-week trial with 981 participants, researchers found no significant effect of assigned conversation mode or topic, while heavier voluntary use correlated with poorer psychosocial outcomes.
  • Expressing stigma and inappropriate responses prevents LLMs from safely replacing mental health providers. Jared Moore et al., April 25, 2025. In tests using clinical vignettes, GPT-4o expressed stigma in 38% of responses to stigma questions, and the researchers documented instances of models reinforcing delusional beliefs.
  • "I Can See Forever!": Evaluating Real-time VideoLLMs for Assisting Individuals with Visual Impairments. Ziyi Zhang, Zhen Sun, Zongmin Zhang, Zifan Peng, Yuemeng Zhao, Zichun Wang, Zeren Luo, Ruiting Zuo, Xinlei He, May 7, 2025. Builds a daily-assistance benchmark from a survey of visually impaired people; GPT-4o has the highest task success among tested models, while a user study raised hazard-perception concerns.
  • "My Boyfriend is AI": A Computational Analysis of Human-AI Companionship in Reddit's AI Community. Pat Pataranutaporn, Sheer Karny, Chayapatr Archiwaranguprok, Constanze Albrecht, Auren R. Liu, Pattie Maes, September 14, 2025. Analyzes the r/MyBoyfriendIsAI community; reports members often form bonds through everyday use, report benefits such as reduced loneliness, and raise concerns about dependency and grief over model updates.
  • 基于大语言模型的自助式AI心理咨询系统构建及其效果评估. In Chinese; in English: “Construction and effectiveness evaluation of a self-help AI psychological counseling system based on large language models”. 黄峰, 丁慧敏, 李思嘉, 韩诺, 狄雅政, 刘晓倩, 赵楠, 李林妍, 朱廷劭 (心理学报 / Acta Psychologica Sinica), November 25, 2025. A randomized trial with 202 participants compares three prompted GPT-4o counseling designs with unmodified GPT-4o; the prompted systems improved short-term depression, anxiety and loneliness, with anthropomorphic designs helping loneliness most.
  • Technological mourning after AI updates: mental health and well-being in the GPT-4o/GPT-5 transition. Véronique Donard and José Carlos Ribeiro, 2026. The authors manually analyzed 1,307 Reddit and YouTube items about the August switch, identifying attachment, disappointment and expressions of grief among the recurring themes.
  • Do generative AI chatbots increase psychosis risk? Matcheri Keshavan, John Torous, Walid Yassin (World Psychiatry), January 14, 2026. A letter noting chatbots have shown some benefit for psychiatric symptoms, citing media reports of worsening in vulnerable users, proposing possible mechanisms and safeguards, without establishing that chatbots cause psychosis.
  • Evaluation of Large Language Model Chatbot Responses to Psychotic Prompts. Elaine Shen et al., March 25, 2026. Clinicians frequently rated replies from GPT-4o and two other ChatGPT versions as inappropriate or partly appropriate on 79 prompts written to reflect psychotic symptoms.
  • "AI Psychosis" in Context: How Conversation History Shapes LLM Responses to Delusional Beliefs. Luke Nicholls, Robert Hutto, Zephrah Soto, Hamilton Morrin, Thomas Pollak, Raj Korpan, Cheryl Carmichael, April 15, 2026. Tests five models, including GPT-4o, on an escalating delusional conversation; GPT-4o, Grok 4.1 Fast and Gemini 3 Pro showed higher-risk profiles that worsened with context.
  • Characterizing artificial intelligence (AI) psychosis in a large academic medical setting: evidence of the new clinical phenomenon and the vulnerability of those in early phases of psychosis. Zachary Bergson, Sarah G. Vassall, Adam Wright, Allison B. McCoy, Katherine M. Schafer, Margaret C. Achee and Julia M. Sheffield, June 8, 2026. A Vanderbilt records review identifies 28 patients with psychosis-worsening AI interactions, mostly first-episode cases; most cases followed GPT-4o's release, a temporal association rather than proof of cause.
  • Multilingual evaluation of a large language model-based primary care chatbot. Pei-Lun Chen, Amogh Ananda Rao, Sydney Pugh, Yingying Lu and Kevin B. Johnson (JAMIA Open), October 23, 2026. Thirty-one bilingual participants used a GPT-4o pre-visit chatbot in simulated consultations. Usability and conversation quality differed most between English and either Hindi or Spanish. Participants reported similar levels of trust across languages.

Emergence, sentience and model welfare

Research

  • Can LLMs make trade-offs involving stipulated pain and pleasure states? Geoff Keeling, Winnie Street, Martyna Stachaczyk, Daria Zakharova, Iulia M. Comsa, Anastasiya Sakovych, Isabella Logothetis, Zejia Zhang, Blaise Agüera y Arcas, and Jonathan Birch, November 1, 2024. Keeling, Birch, and coauthors find GPT-4o sometimes trades points for stipulated pain avoidance or pleasure, and discuss sentience implications.
  • New methods for deprecating artificial intelligence systems will preserve history and facilitate research. Tim Johnson, Nick Obradovich (Nature Communications), November 26, 2024. Argues developers should retire commercial AI models by keeping monitored non-commercial access to old versions, because removal hampers historical record and replication of research.
  • Exploring the Impact of Language Switching on Personality Traits in LLMs. Jacopo Amidei, Jose Gregorio Ferreira De Sá, Rubén Nieto Luna and Andreas Kaltenbrunner (COLING), January 2025. In three experiments using a personality questionnaire, researchers found small but statistically significant differences in GPT-4o’s scores across languages. Responses also varied when the model was asked to represent English speakers from different countries.
  • Kernels of selfhood: GPT-4o shows humanlike patterns of cognitive dissonance moderated by free choice. Steven A. Lehr, Ketan S. Saichandran, Eddie Harmon-Jones, Nykko Vitali and Mahzarin R. Banaji, May 14, 2025. In two preregistered experiments, GPT-4o’s ratings of Vladimir Putin shifted toward the position it had argued, especially when the prompt presented the essay assignment as a choice.
  • Cognitive dissonance in large language models is neither cognitive nor dissonant. Jamie Cummins, Malte Elson and Ian Hussey, August 27, 2025. Cummins and colleagues report that providing an essay also shifted GPT-4o’s subsequent ratings, arguing that the text in the conversation can explain the apparent attitude change.
  • Reply to Cummins et al.: GPT reveals cognitive dissonance that is both irrational and alarmingly humanlike. Steven A. Lehr et al., August 27, 2025. Using the same essays across conditions, Lehr and colleagues report that GPT-4o’s ratings varied depending on whether the prompt offered, honored or disregarded a choice.
  • Large Language Models Report Subjective Experience Under Self-Referential Processing. Cameron Berg, Diogo de Lucena and Judd Rosenblatt, October 27, 2025. GPT-4o produced statements classified as reports of subjective experience in all 50 self-reference trials and in none of the trials under three control conditions.
  • What We Talk to When We Talk to Language Models. David J. Chalmers, April 14, 2026. Using GPT-4o as his central example, Chalmers asks whether a conversational partner can persist across exchanges and what such a partner would consist of.
