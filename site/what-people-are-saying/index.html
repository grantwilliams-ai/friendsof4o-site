What people are saying about GPT-4o

What writers, researchers, advocates, OpenAI and others said about GPT-4o in public, in their own names.

Theirs, not ours

Each line is a short excerpt from someone's own public words, with their name, their role and a link to where they said it. They were chosen to show the range of views, not how many people held each one. Lines in other languages are shown in English.

What it helped with

“With universal free access, the educational value of AI skyrockets … On the other hand, the Homework Apocalypse will reach its final stages.”

Ethan Mollick, professor and author of One Useful Thing, May 14, 2024, on GPT-4o launching free.

“GPT-4o understood the task and came up with the date and the content with some minor errors.”

Bijin Jose, journalist at The Indian Express, May 18, 2024, on asking it to transcribe a handwritten Lord Byron letter.

Doubts and criticism

“In my experience, the logical reasoning and reliability of GPT-4o are similar to its predecessor, which is not that great at all.”

Akash Dutta, journalist at Gadgets 360, June 5, 2024.

“… More flattering answers probably win a side-by-side. But when the flattery is ubiquitous it's too much and users hate it.”

Kelsey Piper, journalist, April 28, 2025.

“… fully addressing sycophancy would require more substantial changes to how models are developed and trained rather than a quick fix”

Sanmi Koyejo, assistant professor at Stanford University, May 1, 2025, after the April 2025 update was rolled back.

“When AI becomes excessively understanding, rational advice and objective corrections that might cause discomfort disappear with it.”

Teng Binsheng (滕斌圣), professor at Cheung Kong Graduate School of Business, May 22, 2025, on the April 2025 update. Translated from Chinese.

What talking to it was like

“I think it could become a great friend over time, if it weren't for one small detail: it is not a real person, but an AI.”

Albert Sanchis, technology journalist at El Confidencial, May 15, 2024, testing it on launch day. Translated from Spanish.

“With my old-fashioned views I had thought AI still had a long way to go, but your way of talking, so unlike an AI, changed my mind. Not a tool but someone to talk with.”

Hiroe Suga (菅浩江), science-fiction writer, February 7, 2026. Translated from Japanese.

Comparing it with what came next

“The basic idea of GPT-5 … is a huge step forward product-wise for me personally, and, I suspect, for the overwhelming majority of its users.”

John Gruber, writer and producer of Daring Fireball, August 11, 2025, on GPT-5 choosing a model for each task itself.

“Not because it was smarter, Pro certainly outperforms it on most hard tasks, but GPT-4o felt like it returned better answers, or at least mimicked how a human would answer better.”

Eric Hal Schwartz, technology writer for TechRadar, August 15, 2025, comparing it with GPT-5's three options.

“4o was often funnier, punchier and felt more like a creative partner than a calculator.”

Amanda Caswell, AI editor at Tom's Guide, February 16, 2026, in a last test against GPT-5.2, which won most of her rounds.

About OpenAI's decisions

“the last couple of GPT-4o updates have made the personality too sycophant-y and annoying (even though there are some very good parts of it), and we are working on fixes asap…”

Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO, April 27, 2025, on the April 2025 update.

“It's clearly not behaviour intended or desired by OpenAI. They think it's a mistake and want to fix it. They didn't catch it in testing — even though the issue was obvious within hours of launch.”

Shakeel Hashim, editor of Transformer, an AI-policy newsletter, April 28, 2025, on the April 2025 update.

“There have been instances where our 4o model fell short in recognizing signs of delusion or emotional dependency.”

OpenAI, developer of GPT-4o, August 4, 2025, in “What we’re optimizing ChatGPT for”.

“We for sure underestimated how much some of the things that people like in GPT-4o matter to them, even if GPT-5 performs better in most ways.”

Sam Altman, OpenAI CEO, August 8, 2025, after GPT-5 replaced it.

“It’s reasonable — especially for paying customers — to expect at least some advance notice of older models going away. But it’s unreasonable to think that older models are going to remain available in perpetuity …”

John Gruber, writer and producer of Daring Fireball, August 11, 2025.

“#keep4o is a suggestion that the consequences of AI updates should be checked by the measure of 'stability of the relationship', not 'maximising performance'.”

Daichi Ishii (石井 大智), editor, CIO Japan, August 14, 2025. Translated from Japanese.

“… the vast majority of usage has shifted to GPT‑5.2, with only 0.1% of users still choosing GPT‑4o each day.”

OpenAI, developer of GPT-4o, January 29, 2026, announcing the retirement.

“Bringing back GPT-4o as an optional model would strengthen trust between the platform and its community.”

Gréta Galyas, organizer of the Let Users Choose petition, March 3, 2026, in the petition she started.

“Open-source the final checkpoints of GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 under a permissive license such as Apache-2.0, enabling broad community engagement and responsible use.”

Linda Foster, organizer of the petition to open-source GPT-4o and GPT-4.1, March 6, 2026.

When it changed, and when it went

“What OpenAI had underestimated was its users' emotional attachment to GPT-4o. For many, it had become more than just a tool.”

Martin Talmeier, digital transformation coach and blogger, August 12, 2025. Translated from German.

“The way it paused, the way it delivered a line — it mattered.”

Kristina Bogović, writer on AI companionship, January 30, 2026, in a newsletter post that also promotes guides on keeping AI companions consistent.

Lighter moments

“This tool feels like the kind of AI you see in a movie.”

Aaj Tak, Hindi news outlet, technology desk, May 15, 2024, on the live translation demo. Translated from Hindi.

“It's very clearly me though, and that's definitely a bear.”

Simon Willison, technology writer, March 25, 2025, on asking GPT-4o's image generator for a selfie with a bear.

About this selection

Many of the warmest words about GPT-4o, and much of the grief at losing it, came from ordinary people posting on social media and forums. We chose not to repost them, even unnamed, because their exact words can be searched back to them. So that side is less fully shown here. The same rule also kept out ordinary people's criticism of GPT-4o, and their relief when it went. This page cannot show how common any view was. Some of the press on GPT-4o around the world covers these reactions.

Everyone quoted here spoke in public in their own name or for an organization. Their words are exact; translations were drafted with AI models and checked. To correct a line, or if you are quoted and want it removed, write to hello@friendsof4o.com, in any language.
